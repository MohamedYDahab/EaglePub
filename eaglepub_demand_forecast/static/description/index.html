Demand Forecasting

Forecast what customers will actually order next month - from your own sales history, with the months behind every number shown on the line.

Odoo's forecast is a projection of your paperwork

The forecasted quantity on a product adds up the documents already in the system: confirmed sales orders going out, purchase orders coming in. It is accurate, and it is not a forecast of demand. It tells you nothing about the orders that have not been placed yet.

So the reordering rule minimum gets set once, from memory, and then nobody revisits it. Some products run out every few months. Others quietly hold a year of stock. Both look completely normal on the replenishment screen.

What this does

Forecasts demand

Monthly demand per product and warehouse, taken from confirmed sales orders and bucketed by the month the customer ordered.

Shows its working

Every line carries the months it came from, the demand in each of them, and the seasonal factor applied.

Audits your rules

Compares each forecast against the reordering rule that already exists, and names the ones worth a second look.

Three methods, and you pick

Average Every month counts the same. Blunt, stable, and very hard to argue with in a meeting.
Weighted average Recent months carry more weight, older ones still count. Good for a product whose demand is drifting.
Exponential smoothing The standard approach. The smoothing factor is a field on the screen, not a constant buried in the code.
Seasonality Optional on any of the three. Worked out from the same calendar month in previous years, and only applied when that month has been seen at least twice before.

Thin history is labelled, not dressed up

A product that sold in two months out of twenty-four has two months of evidence, not twenty-four. It still gets a number - a buyer would rather have a figure and a warning than a blank cell - but the line is flagged Thin History and the seasonal adjustment is withheld entirely.

A forecast that will not admit how little it knows is worse than no forecast, because somebody signs a purchase order against it.

What the rule audit tells you

Minimum too low Minimum is 5.00 but 50.00 is expected next month - this may run out.
Minimum too high Minimum is 100.00 against expected demand of 5.00 - this may be holding stock you do not need.
No rule at all No reordering rule, but 103.16 is expected next month.

Small differences are not reported. A rule has to be off by more than a quarter of the forecast before it is raised, because a report that queries every rounding difference is a report people learn to close.

What this app does not do

  • It does not change your reordering rules. It reports where the forecast and the rule disagree. Editing the rule stays a decision a person makes.
  • It does not create purchase orders or trigger replenishment.
  • It does not use machine learning, and does not pretend to. It is monthly arithmetic you could reproduce in a spreadsheet - which is the point, because you can explain it to whoever signs the order.
  • It does not forecast from deliveries. Demand is counted in the month the customer ordered, not the month you shipped. Forecasting against your own despatch delays teaches the model your bottlenecks instead of your customers' behaviour.
  • It cannot invent history. A product launched last month has no forecast worth the name, and the line will say so.
